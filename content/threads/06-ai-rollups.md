# Thread 6: AI roll-ups, and why we're selling the shovels first

1/
About $5 trillion of US businesses will change hands by 2035, many from retiring owners with no successor.

(McKinsey's estimate, quoted in Greg Isenberg's new guide to AI roll-ups.)

A new kind of buyer wants those firms. Here's the model, and where Shonin fits 🧵

2/
An AI roll-up buys a services firm at a services price: an accounting practice, an IT support shop, a property manager.

Then it rebuilds delivery so agents do a large share of the work.

The clients and the revenue stay. The cost per unit of work drops.

3/
Why the price is low: per Greg's guide, traditional services firms run ~5–10% EBITDA margins, and markets assume those margins are stuck.

His thesis: 30–40% with agents.

His example: buy for ~$800k, take the margin from 10% to 30% and hold it, and at the same multiple the firm is worth ~3x.

4/
The early numbers, all self-reported and quoted in Greg's guide. His own caution: young companies raising money, none tested by a recession.

• Larson Gross, a ~200-person accounting firm: AI processed 7,000 returns this tax season; accountants saved 31% of their time on average
• Long Lake: 18 property-management businesses bought, $100M EBITDA in under two years
• Crescendo: 90% of frontline contact-center tickets resolved by AI

5/
Why an individual can win this, per the guide:

• Funds need big deals, so they skip the ~$2M-revenue firms that make up most of the market
• The tools are the same: Claude Code, Codex
• A solo owner-operator IS the integration
• Many owners would rather sell to a person

6/
The operating model fits in three files:

• target-criteria.md: what you buy
• rules/: whether the agents can be trusted
• corrections-log.md: how the rules get better every week

And one line for the agents: "the reviewer can block but never ship, and the preparer can ship nothing on its own."

7/
That's how Shonin already runs.

Every offer has a rulebook. Every correction goes into a log, and repeats become rules. Every agent decision is logged with its confidence. Anything with money or a client's name on it waits for a person.

8/
Greg calls the automation map "the most valuable document in the deal."

It's our Agent Readiness Audit, pointed at a target. His method:

20–50 anonymized jobs → each task's trigger, inputs, minutes, monthly volume, checkability and risk → automate now, automate with review, assist only, or keep human.

9/

• 100-Day Agent Integration: shadow mode first, then the back office, with a Monday dashboard of margin and client retention

Later, once services cover our costs and a rulebook is proven, we'll buy a firm ourselves.
